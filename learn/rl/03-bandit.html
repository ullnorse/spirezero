Week 1 · Day 4

Trial and error: which opening is best?

Strip the problem down to one decision. Turn 1, the Worm is going to Chomp for 12. Do you go all in on Strikes, or block first? Nobody will tell you. You can only try openings and count wins.

The idea

A bandit is the simplest RL problem: a few arms, each paying off with some unknown probability, and you choose which to pull. No states, no sequences, only the trade-off at the heart of all of RL:

  • Exploit: pull the arm that looks best so far, to collect reward now.
  • Explore: pull something else, to find out whether it is actually better.

Exploit only, and you can lock onto a mediocre arm forever because you never gave the good one a fair sample. Explore only, and you are the random agent from day 1. Every learning algorithm later in this course is some way of balancing these.

Things to try:

  • ε = 0. Pure greedy. Reset, auto-pull. It usually locks onto whichever arm got lucky in its first few pulls. Run it several times; it does not always find the best arm.
  • ε = 0 with optimistic starts. Every arm starts at 100%, so each one disappoints in turn until the truly best arm is the only one left standing. Exploration without randomness.
  • ε = 0.3. It finds the best arm fast, then keeps wasting 30% of its pulls on worse ones. The regret line never flattens.

Regret is the honest score for exploration: how much reward you gave up compared with always pulling the best arm. It is the quantity that trades off against knowledge.

From one decision to many

A fight is not one decision but a chain of them, and the outcome of an early choice only shows up several turns later. The bandit's lesson still holds at every step, but we need one more idea before we can learn the chain: a way to say how good a situation is, not just an action. That is day 5.

Where this lives in SpireZero
  • crates/sts2-rl/src/start.rs: the start corpus. Each record is a fight's starting position (deck, relics, HP, encounter, RNG streams). The env resets from these, so "which opening is best for this start" is the real version of this toy.
  • python/sts2/policy.py: the uniform random policy is the "ε = 1" corner of this trade-off.
  • In PPO (day 9) exploration is not an ε at all: the policy is a probability distribution and an entropy bonus keeps it from collapsing too early. Same trade-off, different knob.
The math, gently

Each arm a has an unknown mean reward q*(a). Our estimate after n pulls of that arm is the sample mean, which you can update one pull at a time without storing the history:

Qn+1(a) = Qn(a) + (1 / n) · (r − Qn(a))

Read it as "move the estimate a little toward what you just saw". Replace 1/n with a constant α and you get a learning rate, which forgets old data gradually. That exact form, estimate += α · (target − estimate), is Q-learning's update in day 6.

ε-greedy: with probability ε pick a uniformly random arm, else pick argmaxa Q(a). Regret after T pulls is Σt (q*(best) − q*(at)).
