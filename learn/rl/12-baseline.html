Week 3 · Day 12

Why REINFORCE stalled

Day 9's agent beat random and then stopped at 76%: attacks first, always, even with a lethal Chomp coming and a Defend in hand. Day 6's table learned that block. Today finds out why the net did not, with three suspects: the representation, the variance of the gradient, and a policy that stops exploring.

The idea

A policy gradient only moves what it can see. The lethal state is rare, the advantage of blocking in it is small (you survive, then usually die anyway), and the net had to compute "damage through block ≥ HP" from two normalised numbers with one tanh layer. Fix the representation and the signal appears; a baseline and an entropy bonus then make it usable.

Suspect 1 · the net could not say "lethal"

Day 9's features include HP/28 and through/28, the damage that would reach you. The decision needs their difference's sign, a step function of the two, and the gradient from a few dozen rare visits never carves it out of a 64-unit tanh layer. Two extra bits, lethal (through ≥ HP) and kill (enemy within one Strike), make both decisions linear in the inputs. With them, the block becomes learnable: the runs that find it reach P(Defend | lethal) above 0.8 and a greedy win rate above the hand-written rule. Whether a given run finds it is the next two suspects' business.

This is the lesson of day 14 in miniature: on the real env the same mistake is a multi-hot of card kinds that cannot say which slot holds the Strike.

Suspect 2 · variance

REINFORCE weights each log-probability by the whole return Gt. In a fight that was already won, every action gets +1; in a lost one, −1; the sign of the update says nothing about the action. Subtracting a baseline b leaves (Gt − b), the same expectation and a fraction of the variance. Day 9 used a running mean of returns. A critic V(s) is a baseline per state: the advantage Gt − V(st) is "did this go better than this state usually goes", which is the quantity the lethal state needs: V is already about −1 there, so a survival stands out.

The critic is a second head on the same body, trained by squared error to Gt. Its gradient must not flow into the advantage (detach it), or the policy loss starts training the value head.

Suspect 3 · the policy stopped visiting

Once P(Strike) is 0.95 everywhere, the Defend-in-a-lethal-state sample arrives one time in twenty, and the sign of its rare advantage is drowned by the other nineteen. An entropy bonus adds +β·H(π(·|s)) to the objective: it costs a little win rate while the policy is spread out and keeps the rare action sampled long enough for its advantage to accumulate. On the real env it is the ENT coefficient of day 10's loss.

The math, gently

For any b that does not depend on the action, Ea∼π[∇ log π(a|s) · b] = b · ∇ Σa π(a|s) = b · ∇1 = 0, so subtracting a baseline leaves the expected gradient unchanged. The variance-minimising baseline is close to V(s), which is why the critic is the standard choice.

L = −E[log π(at|st) · (Gt − V(st))] + cv E[(V(st) − Gt)²] − β E[H(π(·|st))]

The entropy of a masked softmax over k legal actions is at most log k, so β·log 3 ≈ 0.03 is the most the bonus can be worth per step on the mini fight; on the real env with 20 legal actions, β·log 20.
