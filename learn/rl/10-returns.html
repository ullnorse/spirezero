Week 1 · Day 3

Rewards, returns, discount

The reward arrives once, at the end. Every step before it has to be judged by what it led to. The return is that judgement, and the discount factor is the one knob on it.

The idea

A step's return Gt is the sum of the rewards from that step to the end of the fight. With +1/−1 at the end and 0 elsewhere, every step of a won fight has return +1 and every step of a lost fight −1: the outcome is copied back onto every decision that preceded it, good or bad. That is the only signal the learner gets about a decision, and it is the same signal for all 30 decisions of the fight. Separating the decisive moves from the irrelevant ones is what the next two weeks build.

The discount γ shrinks rewards by γ for every step they are away: Gt = rt + γ rt+1 + γ² rt+2 + …. With γ = 0.99 a win 30 steps out is worth 0.74 now, so faster wins are worth more and a stall is worth less than a kill. With γ = 0.9 the win 30 steps out is worth 0.04, nearly invisible: the learner becomes short-sighted. SpireZero's PPO uses 0.99.

Shaping adds rewards along the way, say −0.01 per HP lost. It makes Gt vary from step to step, which gives the learner more to work with, and it changes what the best policy is: an agent paid for HP will trade damage dealt for damage avoided. The real env never shapes; the trainer does, from done, won and the HP in the observation.

Toy · one fight, every step's return

The hand-written rule plays a fight. Each bar is one step; its height is Gt under the chosen γ and reward.

With the sparse reward and γ = 1 the chart is flat: the return of every step is the outcome. Lower γ and the bars shrink toward the start of the fight. Switch on the HP term and the bars drop at the steps where the Worm's hit lands, which is the end-of-turn step, not the step that failed to block. The credit still sits one step off from the decision that caused it.

Toy · what γ does to the mean return

1,000 fights by the rule and by the random-over-cards agent. The mean return is the number a trainer compares between policies.

Under γ = 1 the mean return is 2 × win rate − 1. Under γ < 1 the two policies' returns move apart in a way the win rate does not show: the rule wins fast, the random agent wins slowly, so the discount punishes the random agent twice.

Where this lives in SpireZero
  • python/sts2/env.py: Sts2PufferEnv.step writes rewards[:] = where(done, where(won, 1, -1), 0). That line is the whole reward function.
  • crates/sts2-rl/src/envs.rs: a fight that runs past MAX_EPISODE_ACTIONS ends with done set and won clear: a stall is a loss.
  • docs/decisions.md: rewards belong to the trainer; the engine exposes done, won, HP and block and nothing else.
The math, gently
Gt = Σk≥0 γk rt+k = rt + γ Gt+1

The second form is how it is computed: one pass backwards from the last step, with GT = rT. The exercise does exactly this.

γ < 1 also keeps the sum finite when episodes never end (a reward bounded by R gives |G| ≤ R / (1 − γ)) and sets a horizon: rewards beyond about 1 / (1 − γ) steps barely count. For γ = 0.99 that horizon is 100 steps, longer than a fight, which is why 0.99 behaves almost like 1 here while still preferring shorter wins.
