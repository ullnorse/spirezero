Week 4 · Day 18

Debugging training

Day 11 listed what to log and what healthy looks like. Today turns the list into rules a function can apply to a history, and into a sweep that picks hyper-parameters by the protocol. The bugs have signatures; learn them once.

The idea

A PPO run leaks five numbers per iteration: win rate, entropy, clip fraction, approximate KL, value loss. Each common failure moves them in a recognisable pattern before the win rate tells you anything, and most can be told apart from each other. Diagnose from the pattern, then fix the cause, then re-measure with the protocol; never fix by reading the training win rate.

Toy · the ratio and its clip, one update at a time

Once the ratio of the good action passes 1 + ε its gradient is zero: the objective is flat there, so more epochs do nothing to it and keep pushing the bad actions down until their ratios hit 1 − ε. The clip fraction tells you how much of the batch is in that flat region; 0.1 to 0.3 is the band where the update is still moving but the seatbelt is engaging. A KL that keeps growing with epochs while the clip fraction is already high is the "policy jumping" signature; many trainers stop the epochs when it passes a target.

A sweep that respects the protocol

Hyper-parameters interact with seeds. A single-seed sweep over learning rate picks the seed, not the rate. The exercise's sweep runs every config on the same list of seeds, scores each run by day 13's protocol, and pick averages per config with a standard error over seeds. Two seeds and three configs is six runs; day 13's calculator says how many evaluation fights each needs. Diagnose every run as well: a config whose best seed is healthy and whose worst collapses is not a winner.
