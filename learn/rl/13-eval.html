An evaluation protocol

Every number you will compare from now on comes out of one function, decided today and never changed: fixed seed, greedy actions, a fixed number of fights, a standard error next to every rate, a row per encounter. Without it, a "better" run is a run that got luckier starts.

The idea

A win rate is a measurement with noise. 300 fights at 60% have a standard error of 2.8 points, so two policies at 58% and 62% are indistinguishable at that size. The protocol fixes everything that is not the policy, states the noise, and tells you how many fights a comparison needs before you run it.

With the seed fixed, the same policy evaluated twice gives the same number to the last digit. Two different policies see almost the same starts: episode e always builds from the same start, and only which env plays which episode shifts with the fight lengths. Pairing by encounter, as the exercise does, keeps that comparison honest.

The curve is a sample: both policies evaluated on batches of the chosen size, 60 batches each, as the true rates would scatter. At 300 fights the two distributions overlap heavily; at 2,000 they separate. A five-point gap needs about 700 fights per policy; a one-point gap, 18,000. That is the budget a sweep over learning rates has to pay per configuration, which is why day 18 picks a few and runs them long.

The per-encounter table

Day 5 measured V(start) per encounter under one policy. The same table for two policies is the most informative diff you can make: a net that gained ten points overall may have learned Jaw Worm and lost Gremlin Nob. The exercise's compare sorts the rows by the gap. Read the bottom rows first; a regression on one encounter is usually a bug (a mask edge case, a feature that saturates) rather than a trade-off.

Small cells lie. An encounter seen 12 times has a standard error of 14 points; the table prints the counts so you can ignore those rows or run more fights.

Where this lives in SpireZero
  • crates/sts2-rl/src/start.rs: Order::Random, how the episode number and the batch's seed pick a start, which is what makes the protocol reproducible.
  • crates/sts2-rl/src/envs.rs: episodes are handed out in env order after the batch has stepped, the same for any thread count, so threads=4 does not change the evaluation.
  • python/sts2/policy.py: RandomLegal, the random baseline the Rust bench uses; the exercise's random_legal is its numpy twin.
The math, gently

A win is a Bernoulli draw with mean p and variance p(1−p); the mean of n draws has variance p(1−p)/n. The difference of two independent means has variance SEA² + SEB². Requiring |pA − pB| ≥ z · √(SEA² + SEB²) with equal n gives

n = z² · (pA(1−pA) + pB(1−pB)) / (pA − pB)²

z = 1.96 is the usual 95% line. Pairing the two policies on the same starts removes the between-start variance from the difference and needs fewer fights; the per-encounter diff is the coarse version of that pairing.
