Week 3 · Day 15

Shaping on the real env

Day 10's toy showed a berserker and a turtle made by two reward terms on the mini fight. Today the same four rewards train day 10's net on real fights, on the same seeds, and the protocol judges them by the two numbers none of them is: the greedy win rate and the HP left after a win.

The idea

A shaped reward is a different objective. Whether the agent trained on it is better at the real objective is an empirical question with a protocol answer, never a design argument. Potential-based shaping is the one form that provably leaves the optimal policy alone; everything else is a bet, and the bet is settled on the evaluation set.

Four rewards over one rollout

kindper stepwhat it can teach
sparse+1 won, −1 any other endthe objective itself
hp_losssparse − c · HP fraction lost this steplosing HP is bad even in a won fight; also that a slow loss is better than a fast one
win_hp_bonussparse + c · HP fraction at the winamong wins, prefer the healthier; silent on losses
potentialsparse + γΦ(s′) − Φ(s), Φ = c · HP fraction, Φ(terminal) = 0the same gradient toward keeping HP as hp_loss, with the guarantee

The potential term sums over an episode to −Φ(s0), a constant per start, which is why it cannot change which policy is optimal; the exercise checks that sum term by term. hp_loss is almost the same thing with the γ dropped and the gains ignored; on a fight with no healing the two differ only by discounting.

Nothing moves outside the standard error of 1.8 points until the HP penalty is three times the terminal reward, and then the win rate drops two points and the HP left does not rise. No turtle, no berserker. Three reasons, each a lesson:

  • The sparse signal is not sparse here. A fight is 22 actions and a third of them are won from the start; the ±1 arrives often enough that PPO with GAE needs no help. Shaping earns its keep when the terminal signal is rare, which is the run env's situation, not the fight env's.
  • The features bound the policy. Day 8's scalars see HP and incoming damage but not which card blocks. A net that cannot express "Defend now" cannot become a turtle whatever the reward says. The mini fight's learner could, which is why the toy showed one. Reward design and representation are coupled: day 14's net would react to c = 3 more.
  • The starts are forgiving. Starter decks on floor-one encounters have little HP to protect and few ways to trade it. The HP-left column barely has room to move.

The right conclusion is the protocol's: on this env and this net, train sparse. Keep the shaped variants as the first thing to re-measure when the terminal signal gets rarer, when the net gets stronger or when the starts get harder.

The math, gently

With F(s, s′) = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s), the shaped return is G′t = Gt + Σk≥0 γk (γΦ(st+k+1) − Φ(st+k)) = Gt − Φ(st) once Φ(terminal) = 0, so Q′(s, a) = Q(s, a) − Φ(s): the same argmax in every state (Ng, Harada and Russell, 1999). The term −Φ(st) is a per-state constant, which is a baseline, which is why potential shaping and a critic overlap in what they do.

hp_loss as written is −c · max(0, HPt − HPt+1): without the max and with the γ it would be potential-based with Φ = c · HP; the max breaks the telescoping when HP goes up (healing), which Act 1 rarely allows.
