Week 3 · Day 14

Features that see the hand

Day 8's featuriser is a multi-hot: a 1 for every card kind in hand. It says a Strike is there, not which slot holds it, and the action space is indexed by slot. Today's net gives each hand slot an embedding of its card and scores that slot's eight play indices from that embedding. A permutation test proves the alignment; a training run measures what it buys.

The idea

Index s × 8 + t means "play hand slot s on target t". A head that produces that logit should see slot s's card and target t's enemy. When the net's structure mirrors the action layout, the same weights score "Strike on the wounded enemy" in whatever slot the Strike landed, and the policy learned on one hand transfers to every permutation of it.

From ids to embeddings

A card kind is a category with 560 values. A multi-hot treats it as 560 independent inputs; an embedding is a table of 560 learned vectors, 32 floats each, so kinds the policy treats alike end up with similar vectors and a new card starts from whatever its neighbours taught. The ids come from the cards table (6 bytes per card: kind, upgrade, pile, cost, flags) indexed by the hand's ten ids; the vocab is append-only, so the table survives engine updates.

e = embed(kinds)                       # n × 10 × 32, one vector per hand slot
pooled = masked mean of e over the hand   # what the body sees about the hand
h = body([scalars, pooled])              # n × 256
play[s] = head([e[s], upgrade[s], cost[s] / 3, h])   # 8 logits for slot s
logits = [play[0..9] flattened, other(h)]           # 80 play + 60 other = 140

The body's input is the pooled hand, which is permutation-invariant by construction; the per-slot head is shared across slots. Together they make the play logits equivariant: swap two hand slots and the two blocks of eight logits swap, nothing else changes.

Toy · swap two slots

A hand of five, with a logit per slot from a toy slot head (the same small rule for every slot) and from a flat head (one weight per slot). Click two cards to swap them.

The flat head's logit for slot 2 stays attached to slot 2 whatever card moves in; it has to learn "slot 2 holds a Strike" separately for every slot and every card. The shared head's logit moves with the card. The exercise's permutation_test is the same check on the real net: swap slots a and b in the features and the legal bits, and require the play logits to swap and the other 60 to stay.

What it buys, measured

On the default starts the gain is small. Both nets, 200 PPO iterations each, seed 0, greedy over 512 fights on seed 7:

netgreedy win rateentropy at the end
day 10, multi-hot hand0.8120.53
slot net, 32-d embeddings0.8240.65
either, with per-enemy HP and block added0.8180.6

One point, inside the standard error of 1.7. The reason is the corpus: the default starts are starter decks on floor-one encounters, so the hand is Strikes, Defends and one or two character cards; a multi-hot already pins down most of what matters, and cards-first plays most of those hands correctly. The slot net earns its keep when decks diverge, which is what week 4's runs produce, and when the hand's order carries information (a 0-cost card in slot 7 behind a 2-cost one in slot 0). Measure, do not assume: a structural improvement that reads as obvious is still worth one point until the data says otherwise.

The math, gently

Let P be a permutation of the hand slots. The features transform as x ↦ Px on the slot block and the identity on the scalars. The body sees means es, which is P-invariant; the slot head f is applied to each (es, h) independently, so play ↦ P·play. That is equivariance: f(Px) = P f(x). A flat head g(x) = Wx has no such property unless W happens to commute with every P, which a learned W does not. Equivariant structure shrinks the hypothesis space to policies that could possibly be right, which is what "inductive bias" means in practice.
