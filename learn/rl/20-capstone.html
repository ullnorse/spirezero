Week 4 · Day 20

Capstone

One script that does the course: train the slot net with PPO on real Act 1 fights for a time budget, evaluate it by the protocol against random and cards first, and write the report. Five minutes on an RTX 4080 Super. Then the write-up, which is the artefact everything you train later is compared with.

The idea

A result is a number from a fixed protocol, with its standard error, next to the baselines, with the curve that produced it and the settings that reproduce it. The script produces all of that in one directory; the page is the template for the one paragraph per section that turns the directory into something a colleague can act on.

The script

.venv/bin/python learn/rl/exercises/week4/day20_capstone.py 5 target/learn/capstone

# cards first      win 0.591 ± 0.015  length 19.8
# slot net (best)  win 0.8xx ± 0.012  length 22.4
# report: target/learn/capstone/report.md

What it assembles: day 14's SlotNet and featurise14, day 10's update, day 11's log rows and checkpoint, day 13's evaluate and compare. The settings are day 11's sketch at full size, 256 envs by 128 steps with minibatches of 4,096, four engine threads, an evaluation of 1,024 fights every ten iterations and a checkpoint whenever it improves. The budget is wall time, so a slower machine gets fewer iterations and the same report.

Read the sps column of the log: it includes the rollout, the update and the evaluation. If it is far under the bench's number for the env alone, the net is the bottleneck, which is where day 10 said it would be; a larger minibatch or a smaller body moves it.

The write-up

One page, in this order, each section a paragraph and a table or a figure:

  1. Setup. Engine build id and vocab hash, the start corpus, the net, the settings dict from best.pt, the seed. Enough to rerun.
  2. Baselines. The random and cards-first rows of report.md with standard errors, and the per-encounter table for cards first.
  3. Curves. Training win rate, eval win rate, entropy, clip fraction, KL from history.json; day 18's diagnosis of the run in one line.
  4. Result. The net's row, the gain over cards first with its standard error, the per-encounter diff sorted by gain, the bottom rows read as day 13 said.
  5. The next experiment. One, with the number it has to beat and the fights that takes. Candidates from this course: a harder start corpus from cargo xtask sweep --emit-starts, the run env with the fight net plugged in, per-enemy features for the target head.

Where the course leaves you

A trainer you understand line by line, an evaluation you trust, a net whose structure matches the action space, a reward you chose with data, a run env measured by floor, and the signatures of the ways it breaks. What is left is the part no course covers: harder corpora, longer runs, and the discipline of changing one thing at a time and measuring it against this report.
