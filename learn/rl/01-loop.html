Week 1 · Day 1

The loop: agent, environment, state, action, reward

Every RL system is one loop. Something looks at a situation, does something, and gets a number back. You have run this loop thousands of times by hand. Let's name its parts.

The idea

The environment is the game: it holds the state, applies your moves, and runs the enemy's turn. The agent is the player: it looks at the state and picks an action. After each action the environment hands back a reward, a single number. The agent's whole job is to collect as much reward as it can over a fight.

In this course the reward is the one SpireZero's Python env uses: +1 when you win, −1 when you lose, 0 for every step in between. That's it. Nobody tells the agent that blocking a 12-damage Chomp was smart. It has to work that out from wins and losses alone. That gap is what the rest of the course is about.

The mini fight

28 HP, 3 energy, a deck of 5 Strikes (6 damage) and 5 Defends (5 block), five cards a turn. The Worm has 48 HP and three intents: Chomp (12), Thrash (8 and 6 block) and Bellow (+4 Strength, 8 block). Chomp and Bellow never repeat back to back. This is StS-flavoured, not StS-exact, and it is deliberately tight: attacking blindly wins about one fight in ten.

The uniformly random agent almost never wins, because a third of the time it ends its turn with energy unspent. The one restricted to cards wins about one fight in six. Same environment, same reward, very different results: what the agent is allowed to do, and how often, shapes everything that follows. Day 2 is about exactly that.

Also notice the steps-per-second tile. A random agent in this toy plays hundreds of thousands of steps a second. SpireZero's real fight env does about 800,000 steps a second on one core with the full game. Learning needs millions of steps, so speed is not a luxury. It is the thing that makes the whole project possible.

Where this lives in SpireZero
  • crates/sts2-rl/src/envs.rs: Envs::step is the environment side of the loop for a whole batch of fights. It applies one action per env, ends finished episodes and resets them.
  • python/sts2/env.py: Sts2PufferEnv.step computes the reward: +1 if won, −1 on any other end, else 0. The Rust side never computes rewards; it just exposes done, won, HP and block so the trainer can.
  • python/sts2/policy.py: RandomLegal is the random agent you just ran, vectorised over the batch. On Act 1 starts it wins about 34% of fights, because many real Act 1 fights are easy.
  • docs/perf.md: the fight env's 798,521 steps/s figure and how it was measured.
The math, gently

The loop has a name: a Markov decision process. At step t the agent sees state st, picks action at, gets reward rt and the environment moves to st+1. A whole fight is an episode, and the total reward over it is the return:

G = r0 + r1 + r2 + … + rT

With our reward, G is just +1 or −1. "Markov" means the current state is enough to decide what to do; you don't need the history. That's true for the mini fight (and for the real game, if the observation is complete, which is a design question day 10 returns to).

The agent's rule for choosing actions is its policy, written π(a | s): the probability of taking a in s. The random agent's policy is uniform over the legal actions. The goal of RL is to find the π with the highest expected return.
